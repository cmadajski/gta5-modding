# GTA V Enhanced native motion-blur test — 2026-11-27

Local `local-testing/...` paths in this historical report are relative to the repository root. Those ignored captures and backup journals remain there after source organization into `reshade/`.

This report covers the original post-HUD implementation. The [subsequent pre-HUD implementation and live test](hud-blur-test-2026-11-27.md) supersede its HUD-blurring limitation.

Same-frame captures from the built-in Story Mode benchmark show that the ReShade blur pass changes the game image using GTA V Enhanced's native FSR motion vectors. In split comparison, the reference half remained pixel-identical to the matching Before images. Terrain and road surfaces streaked while parts of the tracked jet and SUV kept more detail. A fixed-camera shot is still needed to isolate independently moving objects.

## Same-frame capture method

`GTA_NATIVE_BLUR_CAPTURE=1` requests four capture pairs after 600, 3,000, 7,000, and 13,000 valid blur-technique draws. On the next eligible frame the add-on saves **Before** from the D3D12 present callback, before ReShade's effects run. It saves **After** from ReShade's post-effect present callback only if the blur technique drew with the same fresh FSR sample sequence. This readback path supports direct single-sample SDR RGBA8/BGRA8 swap chains. The PNGs are 2560 × 1440. [Capture implementation](../src/addon.cpp)

The temporary Steam launch options for the benchmark were:

```text
GTA_NATIVE_BLUR_CAPTURE=1 WINEDLLOVERRIDES="dxgi=n,b" %command% -nobattleye -benchmark -benchmarkIterations 1
```

## Run 1 — split comparison

With `NativeMotionBlurDisplay=1`, the left half passes through the incoming color and the right half runs the blur shader. The table compares the two PNGs in each pair. A pixel counts as changed when any of its RGB channels differs. Mean absolute delta is per RGB channel in 8-bit image code values.

| Pair | Scene | FSR sample sequence | Left pixels changed | Right pixels changed | Mean right delta |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 01 | Night street | 601 | 0 | 11,712 of 1,843,200 (0.64%) | 0.0036 / 255 |
| 02 | Pier and Ferris wheel | 3,001 | 0 | 0 | 0 / 255 |
| 03 | Banking jet above terrain | 7,001 | 0 | 1,310,606 of 1,843,200 (71.10%) | 4.4751 / 255 |
| 04 | Downtown SUV chase | 13,003 | 0 | 1,121,243 of 1,843,200 (60.83%) | 6.6195 / 255 |

The untouched halves are an internal control for the capture comparison. Pair 02 was pixel-identical across the full image even though the callback recorded a valid blur-technique draw. The Ferris wheel was on the reference half. In pair 03, a 100 × 20 crop inside the jet's right wing had zero changed pixels. A same-size terrain crop changed in 1,999 of 2,000 pixels. The crop origins are `(1450, 988)` and `(1590, 1155)` in the full 2560 × 1440 PNG. Pair 04 showed streaks on the roadway and buildings around the tracked SUV. Parts of the composited benchmark FPS text blurred over the moving background.

The most recent displayed sample had sequence 16,281. The snapshot recorded zero unsupported samples, unknown contexts, starvation drops, rejections, allocation failures, and quarantined submissions. ## Run 2 — full-image preset

With `NativeMotionBlurDisplay=0`, all five benchmark passes completed normally and all four same-frame pairs matched. The last periodic snapshot recorded **16,221 distinct valid blur draws / 16,305 accepted dispatches (99.48%)**, with zero starvation drops, rejections, unsupported or unknown samples, allocation failures, and quarantine. The latest displayed sample sequence was 16,223. Peak logged texture allocation remained 162.44 MiB. These counters are a snapshot before shutdown, not final process-exit totals.

After excluding the benchmark prompt and FPS text, the whole-image changed-pixel proportions were 0.806% for the night scene, 0.679% for the pier, 67.005% for the banking jet, and 59.462% for the SUV chase. Both sides of the image showed blur in the fast-motion cases. Two sampled wing regions (2,000 pixels each) and two taillight regions (840 pixels each) remained RGB-identical, while neighboring terrain/road regions changed in 98.5%–99.9% of pixels. This confirms selective screen-space filtering rather than uniform image softening; it does not by itself isolate object motion from camera motion.

The benchmark HUD text visibly softened when it overlapped moving scene vectors. HUD masking and pre-HUD insertion are not implemented. The local `blur-run-02/same-frame-rgb-diff.json` records exact rectangles, statistics, and filenames. Eight matched Before/After pairs were saved across the two runs.

Normal launch options were restored after testing, with the full-image preset enabled and automatic readbacks disabled. FSR Quality and frame-generation-off settings were retained; GTA's built-in motion blur remains at zero to avoid combining it with this pass. Pause-on-focus-loss was restored. Pre-blur settings remain in the installer backup.

## Scope and rollback

The tested shader uses a screen-space color gather along per-pixel native velocities. It samples the already-composited output without depth or reconstruction of newly exposed surfaces. Separate controlled cases are still needed for fixed-camera object motion, wheel rotation, skinned limbs, and switching FSR off and back on. Benchmark FPS includes the capture/readback workload and is unsuitable for estimating the blur's isolated frame-time cost.
